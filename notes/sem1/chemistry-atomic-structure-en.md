# Atomic Structure

## Historical Development

The concept of atoms has evolved over centuries. Let's explore the modern understanding of atomic structure.

## Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808)

1. Matter consists of indivisible atoms
2. Atoms of same element are identical
3. Atoms combine in simple ratios
4. Chemical reactions rearrange atoms

## Discovery of Subatomic Particles

### Electron
- Discovered by J.J. Thomson (1897)
- Charge: -1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
- Mass: 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg

### Proton
- Discovered by Rutherford (1919)
- Charge: +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
- Mass: 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg

### Neutron
- Discovered by Chadwick (1932)
- Charge: 0
- Mass: 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg

## Rutherford's Nuclear Model

Based on the gold foil experiment:
1. Most α-particles passed through
2. Some deflected at angles
3. Very few bounced back

**Conclusions:**
- Atom has a dense nucleus
- Most of atom is empty space
- Electrons orbit nucleus

## Bohr's Model

Postulates:
1. Electrons revolve in fixed orbits
2. Each orbit has fixed energy
3. Energy absorbed/emitted during jumps

## Quantum Numbers

| Quantum Number | Symbol | Values | Significance |
|----------------|--------|--------|--------------|
| Principal | n | 1,2,3... | Shell/Energy level |
| Azimuthal | l | 0 to n-1 | Subshell shape |
| Magnetic | m | -l to +l | Orbital orientation |
| Spin | s | +½, -½ | Electron spin |

## Electronic Configuration

Rules:
1. Aufbau Principle
2. Pauli Exclusion Principle
3. Hund's Rule

Example: Sodium (Na, Z=11)
1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹

## Summary

Atomic structure evolved from simple models to complex quantum mechanical descriptions. Understanding electron configuration is crucial for predicting chemical behavior.
